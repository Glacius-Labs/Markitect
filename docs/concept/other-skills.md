# Other skills

A skill names which agent the owner's session starts and on what task; the agents hold their tasks in their own files ([DEC-100](../decisions.md#dec-100-skills-are-named-after-the-activity-agents-after-the-role)). The tool's commands run anywhere, from a chat, a terminal or a schedule; the agents need a driver, which today is the skills in a Claude Code session.

| Skill | Started by | Does |
|---|---|---|
| `markitect-architect` | The owner or Claude | Architects the project with the owner, in their own session. It sees whether the folder is an empty project, a project without a spec or an existing spec, and starts the spec in one long session that walks every area of the coverage list with the owner, depth first: for each area it proposes, with why and what else would do, asks what only the owner can know, writes the area at once, checks it against what is settled and commits it with the owner's word, saying whether the owner settled it or left it to the session ([DEC-116](../decisions.md#dec-116-the-first-design-walks-every-area-with-the-owner)); then one audit, and it hands the spec over on a fresh auditor's retelling of it: what the system is, the Scenarios as stories, what its users will notice, with what else would do where the owner did not say it, the areas the owner left to it, and what the reader could only guess, and goes through the whole once more with the owner; draws it from the project and has a reviewer verify it against the project, or changes it. The session writes the spec, since it holds the conversation; a large draft, such as a first spec drawn from a large project, it hands to the `markitect-architect` agent, which hands its questions back. It links the real unlinked mentions that `vet` notes. What the owner cannot answer at once, the session opens as an item for the owner in the inbox, and `status` counts the open items |
| `/markitect-build` | The owner | [§5](what-build-does.md) |
| `/markitect-review` | The owner or Claude | The `markitect-reviewer` agent verifies what `status` lists, or with `--all` every Definition, records each verdict and reports what failed and what is a `spec-problem`. With the `parallel` strategy it starts one reviewer per Package ([§10](drivers.md)). The session commits the record |
| `/markitect-cleanup` | The owner | The builder, in a worktree of its own, refactors without changing behaviour, and the reviewer verifies again what that put back on the list. The work comes back when it leaves nothing worse |
| `/markitect-bughunt` | The owner | The reviewer hunts beyond the list and records each bug as `fail`, or as `spec-problem` where the spec does not cover it; the builder repairs, and the reviewer verifies again. The work comes back when it leaves nothing worse |
| `/markitect-audit` | The owner or Claude | Fresh `markitect-auditor` agents read the spec for contradictions, gaps, unclear and duplicated statements, triage the unlinked mentions `vet` notes, and report to the owner; the session merges their findings. One per Package that holds Operations or Reactions reads `doc --read PACKAGE --bound`, which lists its Operations and Reactions first and holds the Package with what binds it and what it links outside it, and begins its report with a line for each of them: for a refused call, a call whose input already holds and the same call twice what the spec decides, `two ways` or `undecided`, and whether its Outcomes, and what a refused call leaves changed, keep what binds it. One more takes the rest, with the lines for Root's own Operations and Reactions, the edges between Packages and the coverage list. Nothing is recorded or committed |
| `/markitect-schedule` | The owner | Sets up cleanup, bughunt and `review --all` as routines ([§12](schedules-and-continuous-work.md)) |
| `/markitect-config` | The owner or Claude | Takes a request on the agents' model and effort, a skill's strategy or a custom workflow in plain words, asks whether it is for this project or all of the user's, and runs `markitect config` |
| `/markitect-tour` | A reader or Claude | Walks a person through the spec in the order it is designed: why the system exists, with its business case; how each Stakeholder uses it, the Scenarios told as stories; the domain; the views, from the context to the runtime, the modules and the deployment; and how it runs. Each chapter ends with an index of what the story did not tell, and the tour with how the project stands |
| `/markitect-catchup` | A reader or Claude | Tells a person what changed since they last looked: for each changed item what is now true and what it said before, the items that changed only through what they depend on, the verdicts and the questions. Why a change was made is not recorded yet, and it says so |
| `/markitect-explain` | A reader or Claude | Explains one thing, an area or a question from the spec alone, and says what the spec does not state |

Only the owner starts the skills that change code or set up routines: their files set `disable-model-invocation`.

**The reading skills,** tour, catchup and explain, help a person understand a spec without reading all of it: the explaining half, beside a reading view that shows the spec whole ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#ideas)). The person's own session runs them; they start no agent and change nothing in the project; of the tool they use only `list`, `doc`, `status`, `vet` and the list of `inbox`, and beside it they read the files of the spec. What the person must not have to take on Claude's word comes from the tool: the items of an area or of a change come from its lists, every item on them is told or named, each statement links the Definition it comes from, and the skill ends by naming what it did not cover, as the reviewer works from `status`. Where the spec says nothing, they say so. A picture, such as a storyboard of a Scenario or the states of a Concept, is drawn on the fly from the Definitions, as every view is ([DEC-115](../decisions.md#dec-115-std-states-the-business-case-views-are-rendered-not-declared)). A person's last look is their own and stays outside the project: `/markitect-catchup` keeps it in `~/.claude/markitect-seen/`, one folder per project folder, as what `markitect status --all` printed, a copy of `.markitect/spec/` and what `markitect inbox` listed, and moves it on only when the person says they have seen the changes. The changed items are those whose line `DEFINITION@FINGERPRINT`, or the state below it, differs from then; the copy tells a change of an item's own text from a change of what it depends on, and gives what the item said before. It uses no Git. Their names and the place of the last look are settled; whether they stand as a decision is open ([OQ-016](../decisions.md#open-questions)).

**Whether the spec says what the owner meant** the owner answers while the architect walks each area of a new spec with them, and from its hand-over, which rests on a fresh auditor's retelling of the spec and has a fixed shape: what the system now is and the Scenarios told as stories, as the reader told them, what its users will notice, a table of what the session decided beside what else would do, the areas the owner left to the session with what it decided there, the technical choices, and the audit's questions; then the owner and the session go through the whole once more. `vet` and the audit support it. A reading view that shows the spec whole is an idea, not built ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#ideas)).
