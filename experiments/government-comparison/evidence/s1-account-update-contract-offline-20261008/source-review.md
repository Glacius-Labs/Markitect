# Independent source review

Reviewed the account-update proposal, its pinned schema member, the source comparison, and the two recorded synthetic test invocations. This review found no material blocker in the offline proposal.

The pinned schema member is an object whose `authMode` and `planType` properties are optional and nullable, with enumerated values; it does not require either property or prohibit extra properties. The proposal correctly separates those schema facts from its stricter local classifier: it accepts only the `authMode` and `planType` keys, requires an explicit `authMode` of `chatgpt`, and ignores `planType` in its output. This supports only a bounded observation about a connection/account notification. It does not establish account ownership, thread identity, or authentication success.

The observer validates against the pinned schema before applying the local rules. It caps canonical parameter bytes and the complete serialized event frame at 4096 bytes, processes at most eight observations, and emits a fixed counter/category projection bounded to 2048 bytes. It does not retain or return the input fields or their values. A refusal latches the observer and the client route treats it as terminal. The route is disabled by default; the session, controller, and entrypoint remain disabled for runtime use. The proposal leaves the general notification maps and copied gate/collector/helper basis unchanged. The account route is after the shared envelope, lifecycle-phase, and provisional checks and additionally requires an already validated own thread, a dispatched turn request, no protocol stop, and the collector’s own thread ID.

The `turn_requested` condition describes the handling phase after dispatch; the unchanged pump/queue does not record frame-arrival chronology. Therefore this proposal does not prove that a frame physically arrived after the turn request. Its account classification is telemetry-only and makes no thread/account identity claim, so this limitation does not broaden admission or authorize activity.

The test history is mixed and should remain explicit. Invocation 1 ran six groups: five passed and the cap group failed only because its test expected the refused ninth observation to increase the observed count. The test expectation was corrected from nine to eight; no production source changed. Invocation 2 reran only that group and it passed. Thus the evidence is five groups passed in the initial run plus the affected group passing on rerun, not one all-green six-group invocation. The runs were synthetic and recorded zero native starts. Preparation records two correction batches overall; the first grouped pre-initial fixture/receipt construction refinements, and the second was the one-line test expectation correction. No production source correction or failed runtime attempt is recorded.
