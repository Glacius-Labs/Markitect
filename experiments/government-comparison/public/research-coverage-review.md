Review scope: public materials only. ## Assessment

The six-cell baseline can compare delivery of a small, controlled service under three working methods and two starting conditions. It can reveal local evidence about correctness, state integrity, compatibility, released-rule propagation, a single regression repair, process conformance, and measured effort. It cannot establish broad user-intent capture, reliable autonomy, or long-term avoidance of architectural decay. The canonical contract states those limits correctly; the coverage map should preserve them instead of implying complete coverage of the expanded goal.

## Criteria and interpretation to preserve

- Score shared product quality against released behavior and independent evidence for every arm. Markitect-specific mapping, model freshness, Executor/Verifier receipts, and Government votes belong to separately reported method conformance.
- Conventional receives the same intent and may express it in good ordinary model/architecture documentation, tests, or plans. Absence of Markitect YAML, ministry names, or a Government hierarchy is not a result-quality defect.
- Count initial setup, repository/context discovery, model and mandate creation/maintenance, agent and subagent work, orchestration, coordination, waits, reviews, interventions, retries, and recovery. Distinguish agent/coordinator activity from human activity and wall time; do not infer cost or usage where receipts are missing.
- Do not treat directory, class, layer, or agent counts or a small diff as maintainability. Use behavior, dependency constraints if explicitly released, regressions, change scope, and reasoned independent review.
- Report environment/policy denial, process/turn completion, structured tool events, task completion, and post-run token or time threshold status as different facts. A clean process exit does not prove the requested work happened; lack of recorded tool events only supports the event channel's scope.
- Six single baseline cells are exploratory feasibility observations. The ceiling of at most two follow-up questions and eight further full trials is an organizational ceiling, not a current grant or a powered/statistical design. Choose any follow-up from observed failures or costs, define a falsifiable question and equal resources first, and report an early interim result even when a method adds little.

## Smallest common addition worth considering

The clearest gap is the first question: all six cards start after intent has already been converted into precise service requirements. If direct evidence for that question is needed, the smallest common addition is one brief, pre-implementation elicitation artifact from the same user statement: each arm states accepted terms/rules, priorities, freedoms, assumptions, and unresolved questions, then maps them to its chosen public representation. An independent assessor would score semantic fidelity and necessary clarifications using one shared criterion. Conventional may provide an ordinary document or tests; the artifact format is not scored. Record its setup and review effort. This is a proposed protocol addition only: it needs a versioned common release before any affected cell and must not be silently added to the already-defined baseline.

For the other gaps, do not redesign the current service fixture now. Card 5 already supplies a bounded rule-change check, and card 6 supplies one controlled repair. First observe the six-cell baseline. If evidence then supports a follow-up, the narrowest likely maintenance question is one common, independently specified change that tests whether the actor updates every active realization and preserves neighboring behavior, with changed components/rework and regressions recorded. It should be selected only if an actual baseline finding makes that question decision-relevant. No claim about long-term architecture quality follows from either probe.

A practical interim report can already distinguish (a) task success and hard failures, (b) environment/policy blocks and incomplete work, (c) each arm's conformance, and (d) measured setup, upkeep, oversight, tokens, time, and unknowns. The final report should make the recommendation requested by the contract: minimum useful Markitect core; optional mechanisms justified by measured findings; parts to simplify or reject; and one concrete, version-bound path from user statement through model creation, implementation, model maintenance, and interruption/restart. Planned product capabilities must be labeled as planned, and no one-trial result should be generalized beyond the tested reference case.
